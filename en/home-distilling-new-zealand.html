Home distilling in New Zealand

New Zealand is one of the few places where distilling spirits at home is explicitly lawful: section 68B of the Customs and Excise Act 1996 exempts beer, wine and spirits made in a private dwelling for the maker’s own use.

What the law says

Section 68B was inserted on 8 December 2009 by the Customs and Excise Amendment Act 2009. It disapplies the manufacturing-licence requirement for beer, wine and spirits made in an individual’s private house or dwelling place, provided they are “manufactured exclusively for the individual’s personal use and not for sale or other disposition to any other person”.

The words that matter: private dwelling, personal use, not for sale. A garage still in a residential property qualifies; a shed on a separate title may not.

Practical notes

Home distilling in NZ is legal but not unregulated in other ways: local fire rules, landlord and body-corporate rules, and insurance still apply. High-proof spirit is flammable, and a rented flat is a poor place for an open flame still.

Measure strength at 20 °C — a hydrometer reads about five degrees high at 30 °C, which is the difference between 40 % and 35 % once diluted.

FAQ

Is home distilling legal in New Zealand?

Yes, for personal use. Section 68B of the Customs and Excise Act 1996 exempts spirits made in a private dwelling, provided they are not sold or otherwise disposed of.

Since when?

The exemption was inserted on 8 December 2009 by the Customs and Excise Amendment Act 2009.

Can I sell what I make?

No. The exemption applies only to personal use; sale requires a licensed manufacturing area and excise compliance.

Is there a volume limit?

The Act sets no volume limit — the test is personal use and no disposition to others, not litres.
