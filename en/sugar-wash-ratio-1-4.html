Sugar wash ratio 1:4

A 1:4 ratio means one kilogram of sugar per four litres of water. It is the strongest practical ratio for ordinary distiller’s yeast: the wash reaches about 12 % ABV, the upper limit before fermentation stalls.

When to use 1:5 instead

With bakery yeast, in a cool room, or when the vessel is small, use 1:5 — it ferments steadier and cleaner, at the cost of a larger volume to distil. 1:3 is for turbo yeast with nutrients only.

Sugar volume matters

One kilogram of dissolved sugar adds about 0.6 litres of volume, so a 20-litre wash with 5 kg of sugar occupies about 23 litres. Fill the fermenter to no more than 75–80 % to leave room for foam.

FAQ

How much sugar for a 1:4 wash at 20 litres?

5 kg. That gives about 12% ABV and roughly 7.5 litres of 40% spirit.

Is 1:4 better than 1:5?

It yields more per litre of water but is closer to the yeast’s limit. 1:5 is safer in cool rooms and with bakery yeast.

Can I use 1:3?

Only with high-tolerance turbo yeast and nutrients; ordinary yeast will stall.

How much does the sugar add to the volume?

About 0.6 litres per kilogram of dissolved sugar.
